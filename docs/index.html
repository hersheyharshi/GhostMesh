    Who can you trust?

    Nodes watch whether neighbours actually forward what they are given, cross-check every advertised link against their own measurement, and gossip opinions. Liars lose credibility when their gossip contradicts first-hand experience. Rings on the GhostMesh map show distrust as it forms.

    Who is about to die?

    Every node runs a tiny logistic model on its own telemetry (battery, heat, neighbour churn). Models are averaged with neighbours each step — federated learning with no server — and each node advertises its own failure risk. Amber halos are high predicted risk. It predicts deaths well (AUC 0.82), though in these scenarios it barely changes delivery; the report explains why.

    Which paths work?

    Delivered packets leave pheromone on the links they used; links that swallow packets lose it. Routes that keep working get cheaper, as in ant-colony foraging, so the mesh learns from outcomes as well as from beliefs. Ablations show this matters far less than the trust defences.
